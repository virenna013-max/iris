IRIS

Colour memory as nervous system communication

IRIS uses colour perception to reflect how the nervous system is responding to stress, load, and recovery.

It is not a diagnosis. It is a map.

What it is

IRIS is a colour-based nervous system assessment and support tool. IRIS is a consultancy framework for working with outputs from the licensed Strannik colour-perception system.

By observing how you perceive and respond to colour, the system highlights:

  • Areas of current stress
  • Patterns of overload or compensation
  • Signals the nervous system is prioritising right now

This information is then used to offer targeted colour wave sessions designed to support regulation and recovery.

What it is not

  • Not a medical device
  • Not a diagnosis
  • Not a replacement for medical care

IRIS works alongside the body, not over it.

How it’s used

  1. You complete a simple colour-based assessment
  2. The system identifies where stress is showing most strongly
  3. A colour wave session is generated
  4. You view the session on your own device (phone, tablet, or computer)

Sessions are passive — you don’t have to do anything.

What to expect

People commonly report:

  • Feeling calmer or more settled
  • Improved sleep
  • Reduced sense of overwhelm
  • Better awareness of body signals
  • Gradual shifts over repeated sessions

Responses vary. The nervous system sets the pace.

Why colour

Colour is processed directly by the nervous system. It bypasses conscious effort and works at a regulatory level.

There is a growing body of research exploring colour perception and its effects on:

  • Stress response
  • Mood
  • Sleep
  • Cognitive load
  • Autonomic nervous system regulation

IRIS applies this research in a practical, accessible way.

Research context

Colour perception approaches have been explored in a range of research contexts, including work related to the Strannik system developed by Igor Gennadyevich Grakov.

These studies investigate relationships between colour perception, physiological response, and nervous system regulation.

These publications are provided for background and context. They are not presented as evidence of medical diagnosis or treatment.

Ongoing use

The nervous system changes over time. Re-assessment allows the system to respond to what’s current, rather than repeating the same input indefinitely.

Some people use IRIS short-term. Others use it periodically as part of ongoing self-care.
